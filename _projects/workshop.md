The [fablab at ISAE-SUPAERO](https://fablab-manager.isae.fr/), called InnovSpace, gives students access to professional tools. During my studies I used them for a handful of personal projects. Here are a few.

## Laser cutter

The fablab's Trotec laser cutter works on many materials, and wood takes engraving especially well. I draw each design as an SVG file in [Inkscape](https://inkscape.org/).

### A flexible card box

My first flexible box: a case for a tarot deck that opens and closes as often as you like. The flex comes from an accordion-style cut pattern. I generated the base plan with [boxes.py](https://festi.info/boxes.py/).

### A Chinese chess set

I play xiangqi a lot, so I drew my own board and cut it on the laser cutter. The board and pieces are 8 mm plywood (pictured at the top of this page).

### Rebuilding a longboard

My longboard is how I get around town. I had owned this Globe Prowler for six years when an accident snapped it in two near the rear wheels. I couldn't let it go, so I rebuilt it from the front half. I also sanded off part of the graphic underneath and engraved my own design. It rides perfectly again.

## Hot-wire cutter

The CNC hot-wire cutter is mainly used to prototype aerospace parts and is ideal for cutting foam blocks. Like the laser cutter, it requires safety training first.

### Cutting a surfboard blank

For my [low-tech surf trip in Morocco]({{ "/projects/morocco/" | relative_url }}) I wanted to make my own surfboard. The first step was cutting the foam blank, an insulation panel from a DIY store, to an outline I had drawn. This step set the overall shape of the board before I shaped the curves by hand.

## Dremel

A Dremel rotary tool with sanding heads works materials with great precision. It is one of the only tools at InnovSpace that can shape metal.

### A ring made from a coin

This time the goal was to start working with metal, so I set out to turn a coin into a ring. It took patience and precision: metal is much harder to work than wood. After several failed attempts I got the shape I wanted. The stone is an Australian opal, set with two-part epoxy.
