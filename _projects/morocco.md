A bike trip along the coast of Morocco that combined surfing, cycling and a low-tech approach: as much as possible, we built our own gear.

## Goals

1. **Surf.** Morocco is famous for its waves. We wanted to surf as many spots as we could and get better along the way.
2. **Cycle.** Traveling by bike let us move at our own pace and keep our footprint small.
3. **Build our own gear.** We took a minimalist, sustainable approach and made our equipment ourselves, recycling as much as we could.

## Preparing the gear

### Bike trailers

The trailers carried everything: surfboard, wetsuit and the rest. We built them from pallet boards, assembled into a frame that holds plastic crates and rides on bike wheels. Each one is made to measure and can be taken apart. The design is simple enough to repair on the road with whatever is at hand.

### The surfboard

The board took the most time by far. None of us had shaped a board before, so we followed tutorials and got help from a few friends. I also modeled the board in 3D in SolidWorks to estimate its volume. Building it took four stages:

1. Cutting and shaping the foam blank. The school's hot-wire cutter helped a lot here (see [Workshop builds]({{ "/projects/workshop/" | relative_url }})).
2. Laminating with fiberglass and epoxy resin.
3. Adding the fin boxes and the leash plug.
4. A final gloss coat of epoxy.

### Shelter

In the same minimalist spirit, we slept under tarps we had used on earlier trips, camping along the coast. The plan was to stay with locals now and then, or sleep under the stars when the weather allowed.

## Route

To avoid flying, we took the ferry, which is still fairly [polluting](https://bonpote.com/ferry-calculer-en-1-minute-son-empreinte-carbone/). From Agadir we rode north along the coast through Taghazout, Imsouane and Tafedna to Essaouira: more than 500 km with over 30 kg of gear.

## Looking back

The trip was a memorable adventure. It showed us that a minimalist approach can teach a lot and lead to unforgettable encounters.
